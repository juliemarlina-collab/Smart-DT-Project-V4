Phase 01 · Empathy

Understand users before you solve.

In this phase, your team interviews, observes, listens and documents real user needs before moving to Define.

What is Empathy?

Empathy is the first phase of Design Thinking. Students go out into the real world to understand the people they are designing for — their feelings, struggles and real needs.

What students need to do

  • Prepare interview questions using T01 Interview Guide.
  • Conduct interviews with at least 3 real target users.
  • Record and transcribe interviews with permission.
  • Complete Empathy Map, Persona and User Needs Summary.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Do not ask leading questions such as “Don’t you think this is a problem?”
  • Do not interview only friends. Find real target users.
  • Do not assume what users feel without evidence.
  • Do not skip observation. Watch what users actually do.

T00 Interview Preparation + Roles

Prepare the team, interview plan and user approach before meeting participants.

Start Here
Activity Introduction

What are you going to do?

Before interviewing users, your team must know who to interview, what each member will do, how to start the conversation, and what type of questions to ask. This preparation prevents weak, random or leading interviews.

GoalPrepare a clear interview plan.
Team outputRoles + interview script.
Next stepConduct interview in T01.

A0Preparation Flow

1. Decide target users: choose real users who experience the problem, not only close friends.

2. Assign team roles: interviewer, note-taker, recorder/timekeeper and observer.

3. Prepare the opening script: explain who you are, your project purpose and how the interview will be used.

4. Prepare questions: write warm-up, main empathy and follow-up questions before the session.

T01 Interview Guide + Evidence

Prepare questions, ask for permission, conduct the interview, then record and transcribe evidence.

Not Started
Activity Introduction

What are you going to do?

You will use your preparation from T00 to conduct a real user interview. First prepare your questions and opening script, then ask permission, conduct the interview, record if allowed, and transcribe important user responses.

Minimum3 target users
Question typeOpen-ended + follow-up
OutputTranscript + insights

A1Correct Interview Sequence

Before: prepare warm-up questions, 5 main empathy questions and probing starters.

Start: introduce yourself, explain the project purpose and create a comfortable tone.

Permission: ask consent before recording. Use interviewee code, not real name.

During: listen actively, ask follow-up questions and observe behaviour.

After: transcribe key responses and write 3 insights while the discussion is still fresh.

Sample Walkthrough

Interview context: Canteen lunch queue

Target user: Diploma student who often buys lunch between classes.

Weak question

“Do you think the canteen queue is a big problem?”

Better question

“Tell me about your experience buying lunch during peak hour.”

Opening script: “Thank you for helping us. We want to understand your real lunch experience, not test you. There is no right or wrong answer.”
Consent script: “Can we record this short conversation only for our project notes? We will not use your real name.”
✍️ Sample answer guide

Warm-up: Tell me about your usual lunch routine.
Main question: Tell me about your experience buying lunch during peak hour.
Insight: User feels stressed when the queue is long because lunch break is limited.

Consent Reminder

Ask permission before recording. Avoid sensitive personal information and use interviewee codes.

T02 Empathy Map

Organise evidence into Says, Thinks, Does and Feels.

Not Started
Activity Introduction

What are you going to do?

You will turn raw interview notes into a visual empathy map. This helps your team separate direct quotes, hidden thoughts, observed actions and emotions.

InputTranscript + notes
MethodSort evidence
Output4-quadrant map

A2What students need to do

Says: copy direct user quotes from the transcript.

Does: add observed actions and behaviour.

Thinks / Feels: infer carefully using evidence, not assumptions.

Sample Infographic

Reminder: Use exact evidence for Says and Does. Be careful when writing Thinks and Feels.

T04 User Needs Summary

Summarise patterns, needs and evidence from Empathy research.

Not Started
Activity Introduction

What are you going to do?

You will convert all interview and mapping evidence into clear user needs. This prepares your team to write a stronger problem statement in Phase 02 Define.

Minimum3 interviews
Look forRepeated patterns
OutputUser needs summary

A4What students need to do

Group: repeated evidence from interviews and empathy maps.

Write: user needs with evidence, not solution ideas.

Prepare: submit the summary for lecturer/supervisor review.

Sample Needs Summary

From evidence to user need

Pattern

Students complain about long queues during short breaks.

Evidence

3 users mentioned waiting time and fear of being late to class.

User Need

Students need a faster way to decide where to buy lunch because they have limited break time.

Important: Do not write the final solution yet. Write what the user needs and why.
✍️ Sample answer guide

Pattern: 3 users mentioned long queues during 12.30–2.00 pm.
Need: Students need a quick way to know crowd level before going to the canteen.
