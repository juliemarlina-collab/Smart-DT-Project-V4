PHASE 01 · EMPATHY

Understand users before you solve.

Observe, interview, transcribe and map real user needs before moving to Define.

🕵️ Empathy Rule for Beginners

In this phase, you are a detective, not a doctor. Collect evidence first. Do not suggest a solution yet.

T01 POEMS Observation Framework

POEMS means People, Objects, Environment, Messages and Services. Use it to observe what is really happening.

Continuous case example: At the service counter, several students wait with forms. One student keeps checking the notice board and asking friends where to queue.
How much is enough? Observe 1 real situation. Write short notes for all 5 POEMS parts.
Common mistake: Do not write your opinion or solution. Write only what you actually saw.

T02 Interview Guide & Worksheet

Interview means asking open questions to understand the user's real experience, problem and feeling.

Continuous case example: Ask the same student: “What was difficult when submitting your documents?” Student answer: “I did not know which counter to go to because the instructions were unclear.”
How much is enough? Interview 1–2 real users. Prepare 5–6 open-ended questions.
Common mistake: Do not ask leading questions like “Don’t you think this is confusing?” Ask neutral questions.

T03 Audio-to-Text Transcription

Transcribe means changing spoken interview or audio into written text. Keep the user's real meaning.

Continuous case example: “I waited for 20 minutes because I was not sure if my form was complete. I was afraid I would have to queue again.”
How much is enough? Write 3–4 important quotes or keywords from the interview.
Common mistake: Do not summarise too much or change the user's words. Keep important quotes as evidence.

T04 Empathy Map

Pain point is the user's problem or frustration. User need is what the user really needs, not the solution.

Continuous case example: Says: “I do not know where to queue.” Thinks: “I might submit the wrong form.” Does: asks friends. Feels: confused and worried.
How much is enough? Complete Says, Thinks, Does, Feels, Pain Points and User Needs.
Common mistake: “Needs an app” is a solution. “Needs to know where to go” is a user need.
Bridge to Define: In one sentence, who is your user and what do they really need?
